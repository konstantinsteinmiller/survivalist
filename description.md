# Survivalist

## Short description (128 / 150 chars)

Shoot the gates to grow them, free caged survivors, and turn one survivor into
an army before the boss turns them all into dust.

## Description (462 / 500 chars)

You start with one survivor and one road. Every gate is worth more the longer
your squad shoots it — hold fire on a `+1` and watch it climb. Gates come in
pairs with a lethal pillar between them, so pick a side: the `×3` or the `+18`,
never the `÷5`. Break crates to hit harder and shoot faster, shoot cages open to
free prisoners, crack weapon boxes for rockets and gatlings, and time your
grenade. Every stage ends with a boss guarding the rest of your people.

## How to play

Steer left and right using the mouse or press and drag anywhere to move your squad left or right. Choose the best gate to pass through to increase your squad size and use skills to help with hard situations. Free your caged units.

### The road

1. **Your squad runs on its own.** You only steer it left and right — everything
   else follows from where you point it.
2. **Everyone shoots forward, automatically.** Whatever is in your column takes
   the fire. You start slow: raising your rate of fire is something you do
   *during* the stage.
3. **Gates grow while you shoot them.** A `+1` gate climbs by one every half
   second of sustained fire, and on deeper stages it climbs faster and in bigger
   steps. Park in front of one to pump it, then run through to collect it.
   **Multipliers pump too**, a tenth at a time: a `×2` door opens at `×1.6` and
   only becomes the better deal if you spend your fire on it.
4. **Pick a door and commit.** Gates come in banks of two — sometimes **three**
   — with a **solid pillar** between each pair. You get exactly one: the moment
   your crowd goes through a door, every other offer in that bank is destroyed.
   The pillars kill anyone who touches them and eat your bullets too, so sitting
   on the centre line costs you survivors *and* the pump. Your crowd squeezes to
   fit whichever door you aim at and spills back out the far side.
5. **Know what each gate is worth.** `+N` grows while you shoot it. `×2` / `×3`
   multiply only the survivors that actually went through, so they pay best to a
   big crowd. `÷2`, `÷3` and `÷5` are **traps** — they cut the part of your squad
   that walks into them, shooting one only makes it worse, and `÷3` is the one
   that is genuinely arguable against a good offer. Reading the bank correctly,
   in the second and a half you have, is the whole game.
6. **Some banks ask harder questions.** From stage 8 a bank can arrive as a
   **locked pair**: two banks back to back with a wall between the lanes, so the
   door you pick at the first one is the lane you run through the second. From
   stage 9 one door of a bank can be **face-down** — a `?` whose value you only
   learn once your crowd is through it.
7. **Two kinds of supply crate, both worth a detour.**
   *Green* crates give every survivor **+1 damage**. *Blue* crates make every
   survivor **shoot faster** — the only way your rate of fire climbs in a run.
   Both sit off the racing line, and an unbroken crate is a solid object: shoot
   it down or go around it. **Every crate prints its health, and they are not
   all the same** — a heavy one takes more than a small squad can spare, so some
   boxes are things you walk past today and come back for once you are stronger.
8. **Cages hold your people.** From stage 2, barred cages stand on the shoulder
   of the road. Shoot one open and the prisoners run over and **join your
   squad** — worth more than half a door, which makes the detour a real choice.
9. **The shield box waits for you.** From stage 8, a box on the shoulder arms an
   auto-shield when you break it. It does nothing until something tries to take
   a big bite out of your crowd — then it **blocks that one hit completely** and
   is spent.
10. **Everything solid kills.** Run into a wall or a stone and the survivors who
    hit it are **gone on the spot** — the rest of the crowd streams past on
    either side and closes up behind. Nobody bounces off. Every barricade row
    has a gap and finding it is the whole point; a wall you shoot down first
    **drops coins**, so removing one is a real choice rather than a chore.
    Pillars and unbroken crates are gentler: they grind at your crowd while you
    are stuck on them instead of taking the whole line at once.
11. **Some things cannot be shot at all.** **Boulders** eat your rounds and
    shrug. They come in two ranks with the gaps offset, so you commit to a line
    and then have to change it a second later. There is no firepower answer to a
    boulder field — only steering, and the stone takes everyone you steer into
    it.
12. **Some banks are walled.** Every third or fourth one arrives with a rib of
    stone splitting the road in two — one corridor per door. You can see both
    offers the whole way in; you just have to pick your side *before* you get
    there. Once you are in a corridor the other door is behind a wall, and the
    wall is stone, so shooting it is not a plan.
13. **Your fire goes through gates.** A doorway is not armour: shoot through it
    at whatever is waiting on the other side, and the gate still climbs while
    you do. But your guns only reach so far up the road — whatever is still near
    the top of the screen arrives whole, so range is something you spend.
14. **Not every door pays.** `−9` doors bill you a flat count of survivors, and
    they charge up exactly the way `+N` doors do: your crowd fires forward
    automatically, so **the door you are aiming at is the door that grows**.
    Point at the bill on the way in and you buy a bigger bill. Sometimes both
    doors are bad — `÷2` on one side, `−9` on the other, one lethal pillar
    between them — and the only question left is which mistake is cheaper for
    the crowd you happen to have.
15. **Monsters eat your crowd, and running into one hurts.** Kill them before
    they arrive — the hounds and the fliers punish tunnel vision. You cannot
    walk through a monster: hit one squarely and **half the survivors who ran
    into it go down**, while the rest are shoved aside and get a moment where
    nothing can touch them — so crossing a whole pack costs a pack's worth of
    knocks, not everything you own. Its teeth take whoever comes close on top
    of that. **They also pay**: a monster you kill drops loose coins where it
    fell, and you have to drive over them, so the pack in your lane is worth
    something and the one you swerved around is not.
16. **Coins do not come to you.** Your crowd picks up what it runs over, so the
    curved coin trails are a route worth taking. Upgrade **Scavenging** and the
    magnet reaches further, until you can sweep a whole trail in one pass.

### Weapons and skills

17. **The road hands you guns, and there are six of them.** The first boss drops
    a **Rocket Launcher**, and stage 2 has a free **Gatling Gun** box waiting in
    the road — both fire side by side. Clear stage 2 and you **choose** which of
    the two you carry into stage 3. A weapon lasts for the stage it was won on,
    and each one changes a different thing:
    * **Gatling** — a hose: red rounds, twice the fire rate, and it pumps gates
      faster than anything else.
    * **Rocket Launcher** — homing salvos with blast damage, the answer to a
      whole pack at once.
    * **Grapeshot** (stage 8) — a shotgun. The hardest-hitting burst in the
      game, and it only reaches two thirds as far, so packs have to be taken
      apart up close.
    * **Dynamo** (stage 10) — every round that lands charges a meter, and a
      full meter is a **button**: a thin lightning bolt straight up your column
      that goes through a boss's shield.
    * **Gravecall** (stage 14) — your own gun, but everything you kill gets
      back up and fights for you. Up to fifteen of the dead walk ahead of the
      squad, taking the bites meant for it until they fall again.
    * **Crow's Hoard** (stage 18) — what it kills turns to gold. Each corpse
      stands as a statue for a moment, then bursts into coins worth far more
      than the body would have dropped.
18. **From stage 4, every other road hides a weapon box.** It sits behind armour
    on one shoulder, and the lock is two **levers out at the road edges**, each
    behind a stone. Shoot through the stone and hit the lever — **both of them**
    — and the armour drops before you arrive; break the box and the gun is
    yours. Miss a lever and the box goes past behind its armour. Ignore the
    whole thing and it costs you nothing but the gun.
19. **Four skill buttons sit under your squad** — the only thing you do besides
    steer:
    * **Grenade** — yours from the start: a burst of heavy damage on whatever is
      in front of you. The first miniboss you ever meet stops the world once to
      show you the button.
    * **Shield** — handed over after stage 3 (or bought earlier): it halves what
      your squad loses for a few seconds.
    * **Frost Nova** — yours from stage 7, with one free try on stage 5:
      everything hostile **freezes mid-swing**, and frozen things take extra
      damage.
    * **Decoy Flare** — yours from stage 10: a parachute flare the boss, the
      minibosses and the monsters all turn toward, ending in a burst that hits
      whatever it gathered.

    The grenade and the shield come back every thirty seconds. The frost nova
    (90 seconds) and the flare (75 seconds) are escape hatches, so pick the
    fight you spend them on. **Cooldowns
    keep running between runs** — dying never gives a skill back early. Locked
    slots show a `?` until the stage that opens them.

### The fights

20. **Minibosses block the road.** Stage 1 has two light ones, every later stage
    has at least one, a second arrives from stage 6 and more come on deeper
    roads. They do not walk past: they **plant themselves in front of you** and
    attack until one of you gives way, and a warning goes up before every
    attack. Beside every one stands a big **sealed cage full of survivors**
    that only opens when it dies. The first ones swing a scythe; from stage 4
    they come in kinds:
    * the **scythe** sweeps the whole lane — there is no safe side, and a fifth
      of your squad goes with every swing, so the only answer is damage;
    * the **roller** comes down one lane as a ball half the road wide — change
      lanes;
    * the **bomber** sprints in, plants a bomb, and takes half of whoever is
      inside the blast a second later;
    * the **gunner** levels a fat, slow bolt straight down its own column;
    * from stage 9, the **warden** smashes the whole road *except one gap* —
      get into it — and the **burrower** dives underground and chases your
      crowd, so the answer is to never stop moving.
21. **The boss is the climax of every stage**, and it stands in front of the
    biggest cage of all — the survivors inside are the squad you start the
    next stage with. On the first three stages it is the **slammer**: it hits
    the ground where your crowd is standing, telegraphed by a closing red ring,
    and every third ring is **charged** — twice the size, and aimed where your
    crowd is *going*. From stage 4 four bosses take turns:
    * the **slammer**, as above;
    * the **claw**, which rakes three lethal furrows down the road — stand in
      the gaps between them;
    * the **healer**, which lobs bolts that burst where they land and puts its
      own health back up;
    * the **summoner**, which never attacks at all and calls in waves of
      monsters instead.
22. **Bosses learn new tricks.** From stage 8 every boss has a **second
    attack** — a ring of fire with a safe hole in the middle you have to run
    *into*, a second rake straight through the pockets of the first, a healing
    circle you deny by standing on it, waves that come up both edges of the
    road at once — and the **gaze**: an eye opens over the boss, **HOLD STILL**
    goes up, and anything that moves gets a beam down its column. The order a
    boss throws its attacks in is **shuffled on every attempt**, so you learn
    the attacks, not a script.
23. **Bosses guard, turn and rage.** At two-thirds and one-third of its health
    (once, at half, on stage 1) a boss **plants and shields** — your fire stops
    landing, so move. From stage
    2, once it is down to its last third the fight **turns**: it attacks faster,
    and the slammer and the claw add a **charge straight down the lane**. And
    the longer any fight runs, the faster and wider it hits. From stage 6 some
    arenas are stocked with **TNT barrels** — shoot one and the blast hurts the
    boss **even through its shield** — and some bring monsters along as
    escorts.

### Between runs

24. **Your crowd turns into coins.** When the boss falls, every survivor becomes
    a coin on the spot and flies to your wallet — the bigger the crowd you kept
    alive, the bigger the payout. Lose the whole squad and the road holds still
    for a moment on a **WASTED** card, so you can see what got you; a wiped run
    still pays out. The first three stages flow straight into each other with no
    result screen in between.
25. **Coins buy permanent upgrades**: squad size, firepower, fire rate,
    **reach**, coin gain, a harder-hitting grenade, a longer shield, and
    separate power tracks for the rocket launcher and the gatling. Reach is the
    quiet one — a longer gun means every gate, crate and wall comes into range
    sooner, so you get more seconds of fire on each of them. Five of the nine
    tracks **never max out**. The result screen shows a peek of the shop, with
    how many upgrades you can afford right now.
26. **No attempt is ever wasted.** A stage that keeps beating you comes back
    weaker each time. In your first session, wipe out late on stage 2 or 3 and a
    **guardian angel** brings most of your squad back — once per stage.
27. **The game keeps pace with you.** Every stage you clear in a row makes the
    next one a little harder; the moment you lose, that pressure resets
    completely.
28. **Something is always coming.** Every fifth stage pays a **milestone**
    bonus, and a star on the HUD counts down to the next one. A **treasure
    chest** under your coins fills up in real time — open it after a few
    minutes for a handful of coins, or leave it longer for more (up to a daily
    limit). Clear stage 12 and the **Daily Expedition** opens: one hard road a
    day, the same road for every player, worth **triple coins**, and it never
    touches your campaign.
29. **There is no last stage.** The road keeps generating, and it keeps getting
    harder in every direction at once — bigger doors, denser packs, more
    minibosses, beats arriving closer together. How deep you got is the score,
    and every run posts your best to a **global board** that tells you where
    you stand: *#1,130 of 2,531*.

## Controls

| Action | Touch | Mouse / Keyboard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Move the squad | Tap where you want it, or hold and drag to steer | Click or move the mouse, or hold **A** / **D** — **←** / **→** |
| Shoot | Automatic | Automatic |
| Use a skill | Tap its button under the squad | Click its button under the squad |
| Open upgrades | Tap the anvil (bottom right) | Click the anvil |
| Leaderboard | Tap the podium (bottom left) | Click the podium |
| Options / language / difficulty / audio | Tap the gear (bottom left) | Click the gear |
| Mute | Tap the speaker (bottom left) | Click the speaker |
| Daily Expedition | Tap the gold chip (bottom left), then confirm | Click the gold chip, then confirm |
| Treasure chest | Tap it under your coins when it is ready | Click it when it is ready |
| Close a panel | Tap the ✕ or outside it | **Esc** |

There is no menu. The first time you play, a short intro shows you what you are
running toward (skip it bottom right), and then stage 1 starts with the road
held still until you steer. Progress saves itself after every stage. Options
also holds three difficulty levels (Easy, Medium, Hard), two music tracks and,
on phones that support it, vibration. On phones the game plays in portrait.
